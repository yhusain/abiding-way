Ways to grow

Six practices of a follower

Jesus didn't hand his disciples a program. He invited them to walk with him. These habits make room for that walk.

Mark 1:35

Prayer

Jesus rose early and went to a quiet place to pray. Prayer is conversation with God, not a performance.

Try: five unhurried minutes before you open your phone.

Psalm 119:105

Scripture

Read slowly and often. Let the Gospels show you who Jesus is in his own words and actions.

Try: one chapter of Mark a day with the plan below.

Hebrews 10:24

Community

Faith grows best among people who know you. Find a church and a few friends who will walk with you.

Try: invite one person to read along this week.

Galatians 6:2

Service

Following Jesus means caring for others the way he did, especially people who can't repay you.

Try: one quiet act of help, told to no one.

Matthew 11:28

Rest

Jesus offers rest to the weary. A weekly rhythm of rest is trust that God holds what you set down.

Try: half a day this week with no work and no screens.

Luke 9:23

Surrender

Each day is a fresh choice to follow. Bring your plans, fears, and failures to him and begin again.

Try: end the day by naming one thing you're handing over.

    From the bookshelf

    Books for the journey

    Classics that have helped generations of believers go deeper.

    Mere Christianity

    C.S. Lewis

    A clear, reasoned case for the core of the faith. A good first read for seekers and new believers.

    The Imitation of Christ

    Thomas à Kempis

    Short devotional chapters on humility and inner life, read for more than five centuries.

    Celebration of Discipline

    Richard J. Foster

    A practical guide to prayer, fasting, solitude, and other habits that shape a follower.

    The Practice of the Presence of God

    Brother Lawrence

    A monk's letters on meeting God in ordinary work, even washing dishes.

    Life Together

    Dietrich Bonhoeffer

    A short, searching book on what Christian community actually asks of us.

    The Pursuit of God

    A.W. Tozer

    A call to seek God himself rather than settle for religious routine.
